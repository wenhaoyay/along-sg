Design exploration · no code changed · 10 Sep 2026

The map is the app, and the map is grey.

You said Along looks lifeless and machine-made. I went and measured it rather than guessing, and the feeling is right — but the cause is narrower and more fixable than “the design is bad”. Almost every colour in the app is the same colour, the typeface it is designed around never loads, and the largest surface on screen is a basemap chosen to be as close to blank as OneMap offers.

All three are live OneMap endpoints I fetched at the same tile coordinates (z16/51670/32530) while writing this. Switching between them is a one-line change and costs nothing.

Part one · What I measured

Five findings, each with a number behind it

“Looks AI-generated” is a real perception with specific causes. Here is what the stylesheet and the data actually contain.

22° Total hue spread of the palette. Everything lives in one narrow wedge of green‑teal.
24% Mean saturation. Pokémon GO’s map runs at 68%.
0 bytes Of Inter loaded, despite the whole type system being written for it.
73/87 Font‑size declarations at 13px or smaller. Some are 8px.
3 Keyframe animations in 2,088 lines of CSS.
01 · COLOUR

Twenty-six of thirty colours are the same hue

The light palette holds 30 non-error colours. Twenty-six of them sit between hue 144° and 166° — a 22° wedge of green-teal — at a mean saturation of 24%. Amber and red exist, but only inside warning and error states. So a session where nothing goes wrong is literally monochrome.

Along todayhue 144–166° · sat 24% avg
Pokémon GO maphue 33–178° · sat 68% avg

The second strip is the actual Pokémon GO map palette. The thing to notice is not that it is brighter — it is that the roads are yellow. Warm against cool. Along has no warm hue at all in its normal state, which is why it reads as one flat wash however carefully the greens were picked.

02 · TYPE

The typeface the design assumes is never downloaded

globals.css sets 15px/1.45 Inter, -apple-system, … — but there is no next/font import, no @font-face, and no stylesheet link anywhere in the project. Inter never arrives. Windows renders Segoe UI, Android renders Roboto, only Mac gets something close.

That has a second consequence. The CSS asks for weights 450, 650, 750 and 850 — variable-font values. Segoe UI and Roboto are static families, so all four snap to the nearest real weight. Four of the seven weights in the design do not exist on most machines, which collapses the hierarchy into bold-or-not.

03 · SCALE

Everything is small, and nothing is large

Seventeen distinct type sizes, of which 73 of 87 declarations are 13px or under — twenty-five at 11px, sixteen at 10px, seven at 9px, two at 8px. A middle tier does exist — 18px, 19px and 22px — but at exactly one declaration each, so in practice the interface has a floor and a headline and very little in between. An app whose whole job is to tell you “+8 min” and “bus in 4” is whispering its headline numbers at the same size as its legal footnotes.

04 · GEOMETRY

Twelve corner radii and twenty shadows

Radii of 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13px plus 50% and 999px, and twenty distinct box-shadow values. Nobody consciously sees the difference between a 9px and a 10px corner — but the absence of a system is exactly what reads as assembled rather than designed.

05 · MOTION

Almost nothing moves

Three keyframe animations total, and transitions clustered on three near-identical durations (0.16s, 0.18s, 0.24s) with no shared easing. Markers appear instantly and sit still. Results do not arrive, they are simply already there. Stillness is a large part of what “lifeless” is actually describing.

Why this specific combination reads as machine-made

None of the five is a bad decision on its own. Together they are the recognisable default: one tasteful desaturated accent, soft shadows on everything, small grey text, generous rounded corners applied inconsistently, and no motion. It is what you get when every choice is locally defensible and nothing is committed to. The fix is not more decoration — it is picking a few things and meaning them.

Part two · What is actually transferable

Apple and Pokémon GO want opposite things. That is useful.

They are not two flavours of the same advice. Apple is arguing for restraint so content leads; Niantic is arguing for saturation so the world feels inhabited. Along needs both, applied to different layers.

Apple the panel

  • Content leads, controls float. iOS 26’s Liquid Glass puts controls on translucent layers above content rather than in solid blocks. Along already has --glass tokens defined — it half-commits to this and then draws opaque panels anyway.
  • Hierarchy through depth, not decoration. Importance comes from transparency, blur and elevation rather than from adding more colours.
  • Type carries the hierarchy. Large, bold figures for primary data; secondary information genuinely recedes. This is the single biggest lever for Along, where the primary data is currently 11px.
  • Colour on interactive elements only. The brand hue marks what you can touch. Everything else is neutral.
  • One systematic geometry. A short radius ladder with concentric nesting, and four or five elevation steps — not twelve and twenty.

Pokémon GO the map

  • The map is the product, so the map is rendered with care. Niantic did not take a stock basemap. Along took the greyest one available.
  • Complementary hues, not one family. Saturated teal water and green land against pale yellow roads — a 145° hue spread against Along’s 22°.
  • Markers have weight. Ground shadows, scale, a settle when they land. Along draws flat 30px discs that appear instantly.
  • Motion means something arrived. Things enter, settle, and occasionally pulse when they are live — which Along has a genuine use for, since it now knows which bus times are real and which are timetabled.
  • Depth on the ground plane. A subtle tilt and shadow makes the map read as a place rather than a diagram.

The line I would not cross

Pokémon GO’s saturation is calibrated for a game played at leisure. Along is read one-handed, at a bus stop, in Singapore sunlight, by someone who is already late. Full Niantic chroma on the panel would hurt legibility and make a tool that states arrival times look like a toy — which would undercut the honesty work the whole app is built around.

So the split is: chroma belongs on the map, restraint belongs on the panel. Conveniently, that is also just Apple’s own principle — content leads, controls recede.

Part three · The direction I would take

Borrow Singapore’s own colour system instead of inventing one

The strongest idea here costs nothing and invents nothing. Every rail leg the router returns already carries route_short_name — I checked, a Downtown Line leg comes back as "DT". Singapore has a mature, instantly legible colour system for exactly this, and the app currently throws it away and draws every leg in the same grey-green.

NSNorth South Line#D42E12
EWEast West Line#009645
NENorth East Line#9900AA
CCCircle Line#FA9E0D
DTDowntown Line#005EC4
TEThomson–East Coast Line#9D5B25

Six saturated hues spanning the whole wheel, arriving in the interface as information rather than decoration — a Singaporean reads a purple leg as North East before reading a single word. It solves the monochrome problem and improves comprehension with the same change, which is the only kind of colour decision worth making in this app.

Then, in rough order of effect per unit of work

  1. Load a real variable font. Inter is already the intended face — adding next/font makes the four phantom weights real and fixes the hierarchy everywhere at once. Cheapest, largest change on the list.
  2. Switch the basemap to Default, and to Night for dark mode. One line each. Deletes the invert(1) hue-rotate(180deg) hack, and gains MRT stations and line colours drawn on the map for free.
  3. Rebuild the type scale around the numbers. The figure a person came for — +8 min, bus in 4 — should be the largest thing in its card, not 11px. Retire the 8px and 9px sizes entirely.
  4. Add one warm accent for “live and now”. Dropped on inspection: amber already means warning in this app, and a second meaning on one hue is worse than a flat palette. The line colours supply the diversity; liveness is signalled by motion instead, so no hue carries two meanings.
  5. Systematise geometry. Four radii and five elevations, applied concentrically. Invisible individually; it is most of what separates “designed” from “assembled”.
  6. Give markers weight and an entrance. A ground shadow, a 200ms scale-in as results land, and a slow pulse on a marker whose bus time is genuinely live. Motion that reports state rather than decorating.

What the type scale would become

40 / 800 +8 min
27 / 700 Hillion Mall
19 / 600 Bus 190 · arriving now
16 / 400 Two errands, one stop, no extra transfer.
13 / 500 ESTIMATED FROM TIMETABLE

Five steps instead of fourteen, with a real jump between them. The current app does have 18px, 19px and 22px sizes, but one declaration each — so the range where a secondary figure should live is defined and then almost never used.

What I would deliberately not touch

The layout and the information architecture. The 400px panel over a full-bleed map, the timeline, the ordering of the breakdown, every honesty affordance — dimmed timetable times, “our pick” rather than “balanced”, the coverage rule — all stay exactly as they are. This is a re-skin, not a redesign. No component moves, no copy changes, no behaviour changes, and the 322 backend and 151 browser tests should all still pass untouched.
